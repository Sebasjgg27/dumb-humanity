Gunpowder (Black Powder)

Gunpowder (Black Powder)

Black powder is the first high-energy chemical — and its honest uses on this ladder are blasting and propulsion for construction, not combat: quarrying stone, cutting road/rail through hills, and mining. It’s also the ancestor of the entire explosives family. Handle it like it’s trying to kill you — because it is.

The formula (by weight)

  • 75% saltpeter (potassium nitrate) — the oxidizer.
  • 15% charcoal — the fuel.
  • 10% sulfur — the ignition booster (lowers ignition temperature).

Mixing (the process matters as much as the ratio)

  1. Powder each ingredient finely and separately.
  2. Mix them damp (wet) — mixing dry is how people lose hands.
  3. Work the damp mixture thoroughly (ball-mill or by careful hand-rolling).
  4. Dry it slowly and completely — then it’s explosive.
  5. Corning (pressing into lumps then crushing to grain) makes it burn faster and more reliably than fine dust.

Uses on the ladder

  • Quarrying/blasting: bore a hole in rock, fill with powder, a fuse, and a clay tamp. Blast → stone for buildings and roads.
  • Mining: breaking ore out of veins (see mining).
  • Railway cuttings through hills — the A3 rail boom runs on powder.
  • Pyrotechnic signals (a powder “line” of three shots = distress).

Safety (the most important page here)

  • Never grind or crush ingredients near open flame, sparks, or friction.
  • No static-prone clothing; no smoking anywhere near.
  • Store dry, in small quantities, away from fire, sealed airtight.
  • Never ram powder with metal tools (sparks). Wood or copper only.
  • Old, damp powder is unpredictable — discard, don’t risk it.
  • It is an oxidizer + fuel in one: once ignited, nothing puts it out.

Validation checklist

  • Can you produce a reliably deflagrating powder?
  • Do you have a safe mixing (damp) and storage routine?
  • Can you use it for rock blasting without injury?
  • Do you keep your powder quantities small and dry?
