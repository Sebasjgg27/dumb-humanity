Hunting, Fishing & Trapping

Hunting, Fishing & Trapping

Protein and fat are the nutrients that keep brains working. Traps out-perform hunting for a reboot community because they work while you sleep — a passive food income instead of active hunting. Efficiency beats excitement.

The trap-first doctrine

Hunting with a spear/bow burns calories and time. Set traps and check them — a dozen snares working the night shift beat one hunter working the day shift.

Snares (the workhorse)

  1. Wire or cord snare: a loop at neck height on a game trail, anchored to a springy sapling or heavy log.
  2. Position where animals must pass: trail narrows, fence gaps, log crossings.
  3. Check daily — dead animals rot, and caught-and-eaten animals are a loss either way.
  4. Small game (rabbit, hare, squirrel) first. They reproduce fast and are easy.

Fishing

  • Fish traps (weirs): V-shaped stone walls in a stream funneling into a basket. Free protein while you do other work.
  • Hook and line: improvised hooks from bone, thorns, or wire; line from cordage.
  • Spear fishing: hardest method; worth learning but not a primary strategy.
  • Stun fishing: ground plant toxins or electric shock — dangerous and crude, but effective in emergencies.

Safety rules

  • Butchering: cook everything; avoid cutting gland tissue (off flavors + bacteria).
  • Never eat: animals found dead, animals acting sick (rabies risk), unknown mushrooms.
  • Wear gloves/clean hands; parasites live in raw meat and fish.
  • Use a gut hook and keep entrails away from the meat.

Validation checklist

  • Can you set a functioning snare in under 15 minutes?
  • Can you build a fish weir that catches fish?
  • Can you safely butcher and cook small game?
  • Do you know which local animals are safe to eat and which to avoid?
