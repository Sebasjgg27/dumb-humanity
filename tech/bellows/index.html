Bellows: Forced Draft

Bellows: Forced Draft

A fire’s temperature is set by its air supply. Natural draft furnaces run cool; the moment you force air through the fire, temperatures jump hundreds of degrees — the jump that separates pottery from iron. The bellows is the low-tech key to the entire metallurgy tree.

How a bellows works

Two one-way valves + a flexible chamber. Squeeze → air forced out the nozzle (valve A shut). Release → fresh air drawn in through valve B. Repeat. Two valves that can’t leak are 90% of the engineering.

The bag bellows (simplest reliable build)

  1. A leather or rubber-cloth bag (a goat skin, a rubber truck inner tube, a coat sleeve) sealed at the bottom.
  2. A wooden nozzle (hollow branch or tapered pipe) fitted at the top, with a flap valve inside that opens only on the squeeze.
  3. A second inlet valve near the top that opens only on the release.
  4. A foot treadle or hand lever to work it continuously.
  5. Two bellows worked alternately give a constant blast — this matters for keeping furnace temperatures steady.

The piston bellows (later, more power)

A wooden cylinder + leather piston with a flap valve, stroked by a treadle. More consistent than bag bellows. The piston-and-valve pattern here is the direct ancestor of the steam engine cylinder.

Operating doctrine

  • Dry air, clean fire — damp fuel kills furnace temperature.
  • Blast into a fire through a tuyère (a clay or iron nozzle) that survives the heat.
  • Air volume matters as much as speed — small furnaces, gentle steady blasts.
  • A bellows is a muscle job: take shifts, don’t burn out the blower.

What the bellows unlocks

The blast furnace for iron, copper extraction, and glass blowing. Without forced air, none of them reach temperature.

Validation checklist

  • Can you build a bellows that produces a steady, strong blast for 30 minutes?
  • Do the valves seal properly both ways?
  • Can one person run it continuously for an hour without faltering?
  • Do you have a tuyère that survives repeated furnace use?
