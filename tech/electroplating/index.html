Electroplating & Electrorefining

Electricity moves metal atoms. Run a current through a metal-salt solution and metal leaves one electrode (the anode) and deposits on the other (the cathode). Two enormous results follow: pure copper for electrical work, and protective coatings for anything that rusts.

The principle

In a bath of dissolved metal salt, the positive metal ions are attracted to the negative electrode (the cathode — your workpiece). The current forces metal off the positive electrode (the anode) to replace them. Net effect: metal migrates from anode to workpiece, atom by atom.

Setup

  1. A DC source — a battery bank or generator (AC needs a rectifier; start with DC).
  2. The bath — dissolve the metal salt in water (copper sulfate for copper; zinc sulfate for zinc; a pinch of sulfuric acid improves conductivity).
  3. Anode — a bar of the metal you’re depositing.
  4. Cathode — the cleaned workpiece, hanging in the bath.
  5. Current on; metal builds up slowly and evenly.

Application 1: Electrorefining copper (the pure metal problem)

Impure copper (from copper_extraction) is full of impurities that wreck conductivity. Electrorefining fixes it:

  • Impure copper = anode; a thin pure copper sheet = cathode.
  • Run current; pure copper deposits on the cathode; impurities fall as sludge.
  • Result: 99.9% pure copper — the wire you need for generators, motors, and electric_light. Electrical copper is not optional; it is this process.

Application 2: Plating for protection

  • Zinc plating (galvanizing): zinc protects iron even where the coating is scratched (it corrodes first). Nails, chain, and sheet that must last.
  • Tin plating: for food-safe containers (see food_preservation).
  • Nickel, silver: corrosion resistance, and later, electrical contacts and decoration.

The prep that decides everything

Plating that peels was plated on a dirty part:

  1. Degrease — hot soapy wash, then rinse.
  2. Pickle — a dilute acid dip removes oxides (sulfuric acid diluted 10:1).
  3. Rinse in clean water — no soap residue.
  4. Plate promptly; a cleaned surface is eager to oxidize again.

Process notes

  • Current too high = rough, powdery deposit. Too low = slow, fine. Find the sweet spot by trial.
  • Keep the bath warm (30–50 °C) for even coverage.
  • Agitate gently; hang parts so gas bubbles don’t stick and streak.
  • Solution is corrosive — eye and skin protection, ventilated area, no metal in the bath except electrodes.

Validation checklist

  • Can you electrorefine impure copper to electrical grade?
  • Can you zinc-plate iron for corrosion resistance?
  • Do you degrease, pickle, and rinse before every plate?
  • Can you control current and temperature for even deposits?
