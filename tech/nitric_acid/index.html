Nitric Acid

Nitric Acid

Sulfuric acid’s first daughter. Nitric acid is the acid that makes nitrogen compounds available — the gateway to fertilizers (feed the soil) and explosives (feed construction and mining). The fertilizer is the civilization-maker: nitrogen is the plant nutrient modern agriculture runs on.

Making it (the retort method)

  1. Mix saltpeter (potassium or sodium nitrate — see saltpeter) with concentrated sulfuric acid.
  2. Heat gently in a glass retort. Nitric acid distills off as fumes/vapor.
  3. Condense into a cool receiver. You now hold strong nitric acid.
  4. Residual potassium sulfate stays behind in the retort (that’s usable fertilizer too).

Why it matters more than most people realize

  • Nitrogen is the world’s limiting nutrient for crops. Before industrial ammonia synthesis, humanity’s food ceiling was set by natural nitrogen (manure, legumes, and saltpeter from guano/caves).
  • Nitric acid + ammonia → ammonium nitrate: the fertilizer that made modern population possible.
  • Nitric acid + glycerine or cellulose → nitroglycerin / guncotton — the high explosives that blast tunnels, mines, and railways.
  • Gunpowder also needs saltpeter directly.

Handling with care

  • Concentrated nitric acid fumes are toxic; make it outdoors.
  • It stains skin yellow (nitrated proteins) — that’s a burn, treat it as one.
  • Same glass/ceramic-only containment rules as sulfuric acid.
  • Never mix with organic matter carelessly — it can ignite.

Validation checklist

  • Can you produce nitric acid from saltpeter + sulfuric acid?
  • Can you identify the fertilizer uses (soil, not just explosives)?
  • Do you store it safely away from organic material and heat?
  • Can you make ammonium nitrate from your acid?
