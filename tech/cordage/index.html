Cordage: Rope, String & Binding

Cordage is the connective tissue of civilization. Before metal, rope carries the load. Senku understood this: the bow drill is a cordage problem first. The entire technology tree hangs on string.

Processing plant fiber

  1. Harvest long, straight stems.
  2. Ret (soak in water 1–2 weeks) or dry-crush to separate fibers.
  3. Strip the usable fibers from the woody core.
  4. Twist fibers together with consistent tension.
  5. Reinforce by adding new fibers before the twist runs out.

The reverse-wrap twist (2-ply)

Two strands twisted in opposite directions, then twisted together, hold far better than a single twist, which unravels on its own. This is the difference between “string” and “rope.”

  • Hold two strands, twist each clockwise, wrap them together counterclockwise.
  • Consistent tension = consistent rope. Teach it slowly; it’s a motor skill.

Binding techniques

  • Clove hitch – anchor a rope to a post.
  • Square knot – join two ropes of equal thickness.
  • Taut-line hitch – adjustable tension (tarp shelters).
  • Lashing (square lash) – bind two poles at a right angle for shelters and rafts.

Splices for endless rope

A 3-strand splice keeps 100% strength. Don’t just knot ends together for load-bearing work – knots cut strength by up to 50%.

What a good rope gives you

  • Bow drill (fire)
  • Bow saw (tool making)
  • Snares & fishing line (food)
  • Shelter lashing
  • Cloth / weaving warp threads

Validation checklist

  • Can you produce 3 meters of cordage that holds your body weight?
  • Do you know 3 different plant sources for fiber?
  • Can you tie a clove hitch and a square knot without looking?
  • Can you splice two ropes into one?
