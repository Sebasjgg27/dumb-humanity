Glass & Glassblowing

Glass & Glassblowing

Glass is the window to observation — and observation is the engine of science. Microscopes, telescopes, thermometers, chemical apparatus, and later vacuum tubes all start here. Glass is also the first material that humans see through: the moment “inside” and “outside” observation becomes possible.

The glass recipe

  • Silica sand (pure, iron-free) — the glass former.
  • Flux (soda ash from plant ashes, or potash) — lowers the melting point from 1700°C to a workable ~1200°C.
  • Lime/limestone — makes it stable (without it, glass dissolves in water).
  • Ratio ~75% silica : 15% flux : 10% lime. Color comes from iron (green) or cobalt (blue).

The furnace

A small glass furnace needs sustained 1200°C+ — it’s a sibling of the iron furnace. A charcoal-fired pot furnace with a good bellows draft. Glass melts in a clay pot (crucible) inside the furnace.

Glassblowing (the skill)

  1. Gather molten glass on a hollow iron blowing pipe.
  2. Blow a bubble, shape by swinging and rolling on a marver (flat surface).
  3. Tools: wet wooden block (shapes), metal tongs (pulls), shears.
  4. Anneal in a warm kiln and cool very slowly — fast cooling shatters glass.
  5. First products: bottles, beakers, flat panes (by spinning a bubble open).

Lenses: the scientific leap

Grind and polish glass into curved shapes → lenses. A single magnifying lens (concentrating sunlight) is also a fire starter, a microscope, and a telescope all at once.

Safety

  • Molten glass at 1200°C — burns instantly and catastrophically. Full arm protection.
  • Never heat wet glass or sealed vessels (steam explosion shatters it).
  • Anneal everything. Patience is the cheapest furnace.

Validation checklist

  • Can you produce clear glass from local sand?
  • Can you blow and shape a usable vessel?
  • Can you anneal glass without breakage?
  • Can you grind a simple lens?
