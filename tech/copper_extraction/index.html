Copper Extraction

Copper Extraction

Copper is the metal of electricity. Iron builds the machines; copper conducts the current that powers them. Every future wire, motor, and circuit needs copper — which is why its recovery matters long before it can be mined fresh.

Sources, ranked by ease

  1. Native copper — pure copper chunks found in certain regions (e.g., Great Lakes). Hammer and shape directly, no smelting.
  2. Malachite / azurite — bright green/blue ores, easy to spot on outcrops.
  3. Scavenged copper — the modern world is a copper mine: pipe, wire, motor windings, plumbing fixtures. Recover and re-melt.
  4. Roasted sulfide ores (chalcopyrite) — the professional route, much harder (arsenic/SO2 issues).

Smelting copper

  1. Roast the ore (drive off sulfur/water).
  2. Charge furnace with ore + charcoal, blast with bellows, reach ~1085°C.
  3. Copper melts (unlike iron’s bloom) — tap liquid copper, let it solidify.
  4. Casting: pour into clay/stone molds — ingots, then wire, tools, and vessel shapes.

Why copper for electricity (not iron)

Copper’s electrical conductivity is ~6x iron’s, and it doesn’t rust away your connections. The wire drawing and electricity nodes depend entirely on a reliable copper supply.

The copper alloy ladder

  • Pure copper: soft, ductile — good for wire, poor for cutting tools.
  • Bronze (copper + tin): see bronze.
  • Brass (copper + zinc): bearing surfaces, valves.
  • Copper’s real destiny on this ladder is as conductor, not weapon.

Validation checklist

  • Can you identify copper ore or sources in your region?
  • Can you smelt and cast a copper ingot?
  • Can you recover and re-melt scavenged copper?
  • Do you have a supply sufficient for wire and generators?
