Permanent Shelter: Cob, Timber & Masonry

Permanent Shelter: Cob, Timber & Masonry

A permanent building is a time bank — shelter that compounds into storage, workshops, and social space instead of being rebuilt every season. Cob (clay + sand + straw) is the A1 master material: free, fireproof, bug-proof, and forgiving of mistakes.

Cob (the great A1 building material)

A mix of ~3 parts clayey soil : 1 part sand : straw, kneaded by foot into loaves and piled into walls. Dry slowly — a finished cob wall is nearly indestructible and breathes (no condensation mold).

Wall recipe rules:

  • Too much clay → cracks on drying. Too much sand → crumbles.
  • Test: ball of mix in hand, squeezed — it should hold its shape without smearing wet. Drop a ball from waist height; it should deform but not shatter.
  • Build thick (40–60cm) walls for insulation. Let each 30cm lift dry a few days.

Timber framing

  • Green timber frames (posts + beams + diagonal braces) hold a roof without nails — lash or peg joints.
  • A thatched or shingled roof with a good overhang protects cob walls from rain erosion. Roof is where buildings fail first.
  • A stone or raised plinth keeps the wood out of ground moisture (rot).

Masonry & mortar

  • Stone walls need no mortar if carefully fitted (dry-stone); mortar (lime) comes at A2.
  • Scavenged brick + lime mortar reuses modern buildings cheaply.

Orientation & climate

  • Passive solar: long axis east–west, big windows facing south (northern hemisphere).
  • Wind block on the prevailing side. Eaves to shade summer sun.
  • This is free HVAC — the entire A3/A4 energy economy is easier with passive design.

Validation checklist

  • Can your group produce a load-bearing cob wall that survives rain and a hard winter?
  • Is your roof weatherproof with a big enough overhang?
  • Does the building stay dry and mold-free after a season?
  • Is it oriented to catch winter sun and block summer heat?
