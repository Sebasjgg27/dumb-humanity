Cooking & Food Safety

Cooking & Food Safety

Cooking is not just taste — it’s the difference between digesting food and losing nutrients to parasites and pathogens. It’s also the earliest form of food chemistry: the same heat transformations scale up into industrial processes.

Why cooking matters biologically

  • Kills bacteria, viruses, and parasites in meat, eggs, and fish.
  • Breaks down cellulose and tough proteins — more calories per bite.
  • Neutralizes many plant toxins (beans, cassava, certain tubers).
  • Safe internal temperature for meat: 70°C+ (pork/poultry higher — 74°C).

Root cellar cold storage (food safety without power)

A dug pit or shaded bank storage holds root vegetables at 2–7°C through winter. Cold storage is not cooking, but it prevents the loss that cooking can’t fix.

Stone boiling (the pottery-free method)

  1. Fill a water-tight container (hide, basket lined with pitch, or clay pot) with food and water.
  2. Heat stones in the fire until nearly red-hot.
  3. Drop stones in with tongs. Stir. Replace as they cool.
  4. Slow, but it sterilizes without needing a pot that survives direct flame.

Spoilage signs — never eat

  • Meat/fish: grey-green tint, slime, sour smell, gas buildup in sealed containers.
  • Canned/preserved: bulging lids, leaking, off smell — botulism risk, discard.
  • Grains: visible mold, off taste — aflatoxins are not destroyed by cooking.

Validation checklist

  • Can your group cook a full meal without modern appliances?
  • Do you know safe temperatures for meat and water?
  • Can you identify spoiled food by sight and smell?
  • Is your cooking water being boiled before use?
