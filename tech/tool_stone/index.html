Stone & Bone Tools

Stone tools are where the ladder’s climbing begins. Mastery here teaches the core principle of every later technology: controlled fracture — turning a raw material into an edge by understanding where and how it breaks.

Material selection

  • Flint, chert, obsidian, quartzite – all fracture conchoidally (shell-shaped).
  • Test a rock: strike it with another rock. A sharp “ping” and a flake that detaches cleanly = good knappable stone. A dull “thud” = worthless.
  • Riverbeds, road cuts, and old quarry sites are scavenge gold.

The two core techniques

Percussion flaking (core reduction)

  1. Strike the edge at a shallow angle with a hammerstone.
  2. The flake detaches along a fracture cone — this is the sharp cutting edge.
  3. Keep the striking platform flat and the angle under 45° or you’ll just crush it.
  4. A glass bottle bottom works nearly as well as flint — fracture physics is the same.

Pressure flaking (finishing)

After percussion gives you a rough blade, use a pointed antler or bone tip to push small flakes off the edge. This straightens the cutting edge and sharpens it — this is what makes a usable knife instead of a sharp rock.

Knapping safety

  • Eye protection is non-negotiable — a flake in the eye is a career-ending injury.
  • Grip so your fingers are never in the flake path.
  • Work on a flat, stable surface; a leather or cloth pad protects your grip hand.

Validation checklist

  • Can you reliably produce a sharp cutting edge in under 10 minutes?
  • Can you make a knife, scraper, and awl?
  • Can you sharpen a dull edge by pressure flaking?
  • Do you know which local rocks fracture properly?
