Distillation

Distillation

Distillation is chemistry’s workhorse: it separates mixtures by boiling point, and it turns one substance into many. Water into pure water, fermented grain into alcohol, wood into methanol, saltwater into fresh, crude into fractions. Once you can distill, you can concentrate.

The apparatus (pot + condensing tube + receiver)

  1. Pot: sealed vessel (clay, then glass/metal) holding the liquid.
  2. Still head + condensing tube: a tube leading the vapor away where it can cool.
  3. Condenser: the tube runs through cold water (or long air exposure) to condense vapor back to liquid.
  4. Receiver: the collection vessel.

The cold-water condenser coil (a worm) is the classic upgrade — faster and safer. Scavenged: pressure cooker + copper tubing + bucket of water = a working still.

What comes out first matters

Fractions separate by boiling point:

  • Alcohol (78°C) — comes off early (fermentation → spirits → pure alcohol = the universal solvent, antiseptic, and fuel).
  • Water (100°C) — the bulk.
  • The first and last cuts contain methanol/fusel oils — discard them (poisonous, the classic moonshine blindness).

Safety

  • Alcohol vapor + open flame = explosion risk. Keep ignition sources away.
  • Methanol cuts are deadly — never drink the heads/tails.
  • Never distill in a sealed vessel (pressure buildup).

Validation checklist

  • Can you build a working still from local materials?
  • Can you separate alcohol safely and identify the toxic fractions?
  • Can you purify water by distillation?
  • Do you understand how boiling-point separation works?
