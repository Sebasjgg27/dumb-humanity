Início / Disasters / Volcanic Eruption
Natural Danger: Extremo (5/5)

Volcanic Eruption

1 Priority Actions

  1. 1 Evacuate immediately if ordered — do not wait to see the eruption
  2. 2 Move upwind and uphill — ash flows follow valleys and wind direction
  3. 3 Protect lungs: wear N95 mask, wet cloth, or multiple layers of fabric
  4. 4 Close all windows and doors — seal gaps with wet towels
  5. 5 Avoid river valleys — lahars (volcanic mudflows) follow water channels

Key Dangers

  • Pyroclastic flows — superheated gas and rock moving at 700+ km/h
  • Lahars — volcanic mudflows traveling down river valleys at high speed
  • Ash fall — collapses roofs, contaminates water, destroys crops
  • Toxic gases — sulfur dioxide, carbon monoxide, hydrogen sulfide
  • Lava flows — slow but unstoppable, everything in path is destroyed

Shelter

Evacuation zone is the only real shelter. If caught: sealed building with no windows, highest floor. Place wet towels under doors and over ventilation. Ash is extremely heavy when wet — clear flat roofs if safe.

Water

Collect rainwater BEFORE ash fall begins. After eruption, all outdoor water is contaminated with ash and chemicals. Rainwater collected during heavy ash fall is also contaminated — ash makes water acidic.

Fire

Volcanic lightning can start fires. Lava ignites everything it contacts. Use fire only if area is confirmed safe from flows. Emergency fire for warmth or cooking only in clear air.

Food

Stockpiled canned goods — seal food from ash contamination. Greenhouses may protect some crops if ash fall is light. Ash-fallout crops are unsafe — chemicals leach into food.

Signals & Rescue

  • Bright fabric on rooftop — visible through light ash
  • Mirror flash from open ground toward clear sky
  • Whistle blasts for search teams in ash-darkened conditions
  • Three fires on high ground — visible above ash layer
  • Radio broadcast if power available

Common Mistakes

  • Staying to watch the eruption — pyroclastic flows are faster than you
  • Driving in heavy ash — engines clog, visibility drops to zero
  • Opening windows during ash fall — ash enters and is impossible to clean
  • Driving through river valleys — lahars are invisible until on top of you
  • Ignoring evacuation orders because the eruption seems small
