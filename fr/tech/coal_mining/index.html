Coal Mining

Charcoal is for the forge; coal is for the age. Wood simply cannot feed blast furnaces, steam engines, and whole towns. Coal is fossil sunlight — dense, abundant energy that the industrial seed needs to scale. If you cannot mine it, your industry hits a ceiling a century early.

What coal is and which kind you want

Ranked by age and heat value:

  • Peat — young, wet, poor. Dry it first; it’s a start.
  • Lignite (brown coal) — soft, smoky, low heat.
  • Bituminous — the workhorse: burns hot and long.
  • Anthracite — hard, clean, very hot; the prize.

For the blast furnace you want coke (see below), made from bituminous coal.

Finding it

  • Look for exposed outcrops on hillsides, riverbanks, and road cuts.
  • Test pits: dig down where the geology looks layered; coal often sits above clay and below rock.
  • Black-stained streams and sooty soil in a settled area hint at a seam below.

Starting: the surface mine

  1. Stripping — remove the soil and rock covering the seam (overburden).
  2. Mine the coal openly, working the face back.
  3. Haul with wheelbarrows and carts; rails inside the pit make hauling far easier.

Only go underground when the seam dips below reach.

Going underground (when you must)

  • Two shafts or adits are the first rule: fresh air down one, bad air up the other (see mining).
  • Timber props every step of the way — roof collapse is the silent killer.
  • Bratrice cloth (canvas) directs ventilation to the working face.
  • Keep the pit drained; water collects fast. A windlass and buckets, then a steam-powered pump once engines exist.

The four underground killers

  1. Firedamp (methane) — accumulates at the roof, explodes on any spark. Ventilate; test with a flame in a safe place; no naked lights near the roof.
  2. Blackdamp (CO2) — sinks to the floor, suffocates silently. A candle goes out — that’s the warning: get out.
  3. Roof collapse — timber up; inspect after every blast.
  4. Water — floods in. Know the history of the seam before going deep.

Making coke (the blast-furnace fuel)

Bake bituminous coal in a mound or oven, with limited air, until the volatiles burn off. What remains is coke — nearly pure carbon, strong enough to support a blast furnace charge without collapsing. The blast furnace (see iron_smelting) prefers coke over raw coal.

Safety and honesty

  • Coal dust and methane make pits genuinely lethal. This guide is a summary, not a license — proceed carefully, ventilate always, and lose no one to a cave-in or a gas pocket.
  • No naked flames in a gassy pit. Ever.
  • Miners need rest, clean water, and daylight — mining is brutal, plan for the humans.

Validation checklist

  • Can you locate and open a working coal seam?
  • Can you ventilate and support an underground working?
  • Can you produce coke for the blast furnace?
  • Do you have survival rules for gas, water, and roof collapse?
