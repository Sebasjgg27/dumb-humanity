# Mining & Ore Extraction

Every furnace in the ladder eats ore and fuel. Surface deposits run out fast — the
step up to *mining* (going underground) multiplies what's available, but underground
is where carelessness kills. Mining's three eternal problems: **collapse, water,
and air** — solve those and the shaft is safe.

## The three killers, and their cures

| Killer     | Cure                                     |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Collapse   | Timber shoring, narrow adits, inspect daily |
| Water      | Pump (water wheel → steam) the shaft continuously |
| Bad air    | Ventilation shafts + air circulation (fires create draft); test with a candle — low flame = danger |
| *Plus*     | Gas (methane/CO₂) — candle test always before entering |

## Surface first

- **Open-cast**: strip away overburden from shallow ore. Fast, safe, high yield.
- **Adits** (horizontal tunnels) into hillsides: drainage is free, ventilation easy.
- Only when surface is exhausted go vertical shafts (expensive, dangerous).

## Tools and technique

- Steel drills and picks from the [forge](../iron_smelting/).
- **Blasting** with [gunpowder](../gunpowder/): drill a shot-hole,
  charge, fuse, blast, ventilate the smoke, then muck out.
- Crush and sort ore at the surface; reject waste rock before hauling.
- A water wheel at the shaft mouth drives pumps and the ore crusher (see [water
  wheel](../water_wheel/)).

## The mine is infrastructure

- A mine is a *long-term* asset — timber it properly and it produces for decades.
- Map it: every tunnel drawn, every known void recorded. A lost map is a death trap
  later.
- **Never** re-enter a collapsed or flooded mine after a disaster.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Can you mine safely (shoring, ventilation, pump)?
- [ ] Can you blast rock with gunpowder without injury?
- [ ] Can you crush, sort, and haul ore to the surface?
- [ ] Do you keep an up-to-date mine map?
