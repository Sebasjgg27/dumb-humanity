# Electricity & the Generator

Electricity is the medium of the modern world — and it's *obtainable* at A2, because
its foundation is ancient physics: move a magnet near a wire and current flows
(electromagnetic induction, 1831). Once you can spin a coil in a magnetic field,
you have light, motors, batteries to store it, and the communications tree.

## The generator (the one-machine-to-understand)

A generator = a **coil of copper wire** rotating inside (or around) a **magnetic
field**. The rotating coil produces alternating current (AC); a **commutator** (a
rotating switch) converts it to direct current (DC) for batteries/electroplating.

Build recipe:

1. **Wire**: [drawn copper](../wire_drawing/), wound into many-turn
   coils on iron cores (laminated iron concentrates the magnetic field).
2. **Magnet**: permanent magnets from scavenged magnets, or an **electromagnet**
   powered by a small battery + iron core (self-excited generators boot up with
   a small magnet — the "flash" field).
3. **Rotation**: a [water wheel](../water_wheel/), steam engine, or
   windmill turns the armature.
4. **Output**: AC → light bulbs / motors; DC → battery charging.

## Light, the social engine

Electric light extends the workday. Carbon-filament bulbs (A2-era, ~1879 tech) are
achievable with [glass](../glassblowing/) + carbonized bamboo/thread
in vacuum. A workshop lit at night doubles production.

## Safety

- Electrical burns and shock are real, silent killers. Insulate, dry-hands, never
  touch live circuit with wet skin.
- Generator wiring running hot = fire risk. Fuse everything.
- AC voltage can be lethal — build systems for low voltage first (12V).
