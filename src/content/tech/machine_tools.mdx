# Machine Tools

This is the hinge of the whole ladder. A single lathe is a curiosity; a *family* of
machine tools is the factory that builds every other machine — the steam engine, the
generator, the pumps, the railway — and then builds **more machine tools**. Once this
shop exists, the industrial age is no longer a matter of *if* but of *how fast*.

## The self-replicating principle

Machine tools are machines that cut and shape metal (and wood) precisely. Because they
can make their own parts, the shop grows on itself: each new machine makes the next
machine better.

The critical insight: **a hard, rigid tool cutting a softer workpiece, moved at a
controlled rate, produces accuracy that no hand can match.**

## The essential family (in build order)

1. **Metal-turning lathe** (already on the ladder — see [lathe](../lathe/)).
   The foundation. With it you can make shafts, axles, and pulleys.
2. **Screw-cutting lathe** — the lathe with a lead screw and change gears. This is the
   secret to *standardized threads*, which lets parts from different workshops fit
   together. Standard threads are a quiet miracle of the industrial world.
3. **Drill press** — accurate holes at right angles (needed for bearing housings,
   flanges, and frames).
4. **Shaper or planer** — cuts flat surfaces on steel (lathe parts must seat flat).
5. **Grinder** — the final word in accuracy: grinding against a true surface corrects
   what cutting leaves behind.

Each of these is buildable from cast iron, steel, and a lathe — given time.

## What makes parts "interchangeable"

Metrology (see [metrology](../metrology/)) turns production from
"fit each piece by trial" into "any A fits any B." The payoff is enormous:

- A broken part is **replaced**, not repaired by hand.
- Different shops can each make one part, and assembly still works.
- The steam engine and railway become *maintainable*, not just buildable.

## Powering the shop

- **Water wheel** for heavy cuts: the [water_wheel](../water_wheel/)
  powers shafts, belts, and line-shafting that run the whole machine shop.
- **Muscle** (treadle/crank) for light work and finishing.
- Later, steam.

## Safety (a working shop is a dangerous one)

- **No loose clothing, hair, or jewelry** around rotating shafts and belts.
- Use cutting fluid (oil-water mix) — hot chips and hardened tool edges burn and cut.
- Lock workpieces; never reach across a running machine.
- Keep belts and pulleys guarded; line-shafting has killed more people than any tool.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Can you turn accurate shafts and cylinders on a metal lathe?
- [ ] Can you cut standardized screw threads?
- [ ] Can you drill true holes and surface-finish steel?
- [ ] Can the shop reproduce and improve its own machines?
