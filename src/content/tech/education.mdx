# Education & Knowledge Transmission

The most durable storage format is **a trained person**. Machines corrode and pages burn,
but a person who has practiced a skill can rebuild it anywhere. This guide is about making
knowledge transmission a *system* instead of an accident — because a civilization that
cannot teach itself will lose itself within one generation.

## The redundancy doctrine applied to people

Never let a critical skill live in one head. For every skill this manual depends on:

- **Three people** must be able to perform it.
- One of them must be able to **teach** it.
- Knowledge must exist in **written form** as backup.

Losing one person should never lose a technology.

## The apprenticeship model

Physical skills (smithing, brewing, building) are best taught by working alongside a
master:

1. **Watch** — the learner observes.
2. **Assist** — the learner handles materials and tools.
3. **Perform under supervision** — the learner does the work, master corrects.
4. **Perform alone** — the learner teaches the next apprentice (this closes the loop).

## The school model

For literacy, numeracy, and theory, a school is more efficient than one-on-one teaching:

- **Stage 1 — Reading, writing, arithmetic.** Everything else depends on these. Reading
  is the skill that lets anyone access the archive.
- **Stage 2 — Ladder foundations.** Teach the A0–A1 material: water, fire, shelter,
  first aid, hygiene, food. Practical, not abstract.
- **Stage 3 — Tracks.** Students branch into the trades: chemistry, metallurgy,
  engineering, agriculture, medicine — each following the relevant guides.

## Teaching techniques that work at any tech level

- **Write everything down.** If it isn't written, it isn't preserved. Copy guides by
  hand; use printing once available.
- **Test recall.** Have students explain procedures from memory; memory is the true
  storage medium.
- **Teach by doing.** A day at the forge teaches more than a week of lectures.
- **Keep the manual sacred.** Designate archive copies; repair and re-copy them.
- **Rotate.** Cross-train people so the tree has no single point of failure.

## What everyone must memorize

Before all else, drill these so they become reflexive:

- Clean water prevents more deaths than any medicine.
- Infection: wash, boil, cover, separate the sick.
- Fire: contained and never left.
- Food safety: cook, dry, salt, store off the ground.
- The three-phase response to injury: stop bleeding, clean wound, prevent infection.

## The quiet rule

**Teach before you need it.** Knowledge taught in calm times is what saves lives in
crisis times. The school is infrastructure, exactly like the water system.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Does every critical skill have at least three trained people?
- [ ] Is there a functioning reading/writing/arithmetic program?
- [ ] Are the core ladder skills documented and copied?
- [ ] Is there a designated archive, with duplicates stored separately?
