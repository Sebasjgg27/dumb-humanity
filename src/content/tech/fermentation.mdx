# Fermentation (Brewing, Baking & Vinegar)

Yeast turns sugar into alcohol and carbon dioxide. That one trick gives you **safer
drinks, longer-lasting calories, leavened bread, and vinegar** — and it's the oldest
food-preservation technology in the book after drying and salting. It costs no fuel,
just time.

## The core principle

Wild or cultured yeast eats sugar and excretes ethanol + CO2. Different substrates
give different products:

- **Fruit** → wine (or cider, mead).
- **Grain** (malted) → beer.
- **Honey + water** → mead.
- **Flour + water** → bread (CO2 puffs the dough; the alcohol evaporates in baking).

## Making a basic brew

1. **Prepare the sugar source.** Crush/mash fruit, or malt grain (let it sprout, dry,
   then crack). Add clean water.
2. **Pitch yeast.** Save lees from a previous brew, or catch wild yeast by leaving the
   must exposed briefly. Commercial/salvaged yeast is a huge shortcut.
3. **Ferment in an open-but-covered vessel** — clean cloth over the mouth keeps bugs
   out while letting CO2 escape. A proper airlock is better.
4. **Give it time and warmth.** 15–25 °C, days to a couple of weeks. Cooler = slower
   but cleaner.
5. **Rack it** (siphon off the clear liquid) to separate from sediment.

## Bread without an oven's thermometer

1. Mix flour and water with a spoonful of saved starter (or yeast).
2. Knead, cover, let it rise until doubled.
3. Bake in a covered pot or stone oven. Even a flat skillet makes good flatbread.

## Vinegar: the accidental preservative

Let finished alcohol sit exposed to air and **Acetobacter** bacteria will turn it into
**acetic acid** — vinegar. Vinegar preserves food (pickling), cleans wounds, and removes
lime scale. This is a preservative worth keeping on the shelf.

## Safety and honesty

- **Never seal a fermenting vessel airtight** — pressure can burst it.
- Keep everything clean; infections produce off-flavors and, rarely, harmful results.
  Trust your nose: foul = discard.
- Fermentation **does not sanitize water by itself** — start with boiled or clean water.
- Distilling concentrates alcohol — see [distillation](../distillation/)
  for the methanol dangers before anyone tries to make spirits.
