# Explosives (High)

Black powder *deflagrates* — burns fast. High explosives *detonate* — release energy
faster than the speed of sound in the material, shattering rock in one violent pulse.
That difference is what blasts tunnels, mines, and railway cuttings that powder alone
struggles with. It is also the difference between "dangerous" and "kill you twice."

**This is the most dangerous page in the manual. Read it twice before touching anything.**

## The two classics

**Nitroglycerin** (the liquid shocker):
1. Mix glycerine into a chilled mixture of nitric acid and sulfuric acid.
2. The sulfuric acid soaks up water; the nitrate group attaches to glycerine.
3. A heavy, oily, pale liquid forms. It is **absurdly sensitive** — a jolt, a slam,
   even rapid cooling can set it off.

**Guncotton (nitrocellulose)** (the safer cousin):
- Cellulose (cotton, wood fiber) + nitric acid → nitrocellulose. A fibrous, flaky
  material that burns violently and detonates when primed. Much more stable to handle
  than nitroglycerin liquid.

## Taming the nightmare: dynamite

Liquid nitroglycerin is not something anyone should carry. Alfred Nobel's fix: soak it
into a porous absorbent (**diatomaceous earth / kieselguhr**), forming a stiff paste —
**dynamite** — that needs a blasting cap to set off. Never store, move, or use liquid
nitroglycerin. Make it, stabilize it into dynamite immediately, and use it fresh.

## Detonating it: the blasting cap

High explosives do not respond to a match. They need a **shock**:

- **Mercury fulminate** (mercury + alcohol + nitric acid) detonates when struck —
  the classic cap initiator.
- **Gunpowder fuse + cap**: light the fuse; the powder's flash ignites the fulminate;
  the cap's shock detonates the main charge.

## The safest high explosive: ammonium nitrate

Ammonium nitrate (nitric acid + ammonia — see [nitric_acid](/dumb-humanity/tech/nitric_acid))
does not detonate by itself under normal handling. Mixed with a fuel (diesel, kerosene)
and given a strong primer (a blasting cap, ideally boosted), it produces the mining
explosive known as ANFO. This is the industry standard for a reason: it is cheap,
stable, and powerful.

## Operating rules (non-negotiable)

- **Make fresh, use fresh.** Old explosives migrate, crystallize, and become deadly.
- Never store liquid nitroglycerin. Ever.
- Keep acids and organic materials strictly separate until the moment of reaction.
- Work outdoors, far from people, on a clean bench. No sparks, no flame, no metal tools.
- One person in the blast zone; clear all bystanders; sound warning signals.
- Bury and damp-destroy anything suspicious rather than handling it.

## Uses on the ladder

- **Mining**: breaking ore and coal (see [mining](/dumb-humanity/tech/mining) and
  [coal_mining](/dumb-humanity/tech/coal_mining)).
- **Railway cuttings** through hills and tunnels.
- **Quarrying** building stone for the A3 infrastructure boom.
