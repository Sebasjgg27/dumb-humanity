# Community Governance & Knowledge Transfer

Everything on this ladder is useless if the knowledge dies with the people who knew
it. Institutions are technology too — arguably the most important kind, because
knowledge is the only resource that can't be scavenged. Governance, education, and
records are what make knowledge *compound*.

## The core institutions to build

1. **Written records** — the highest-leverage invention. A village chronicle, a
   calendar, a seed/stock inventory, a "what we learned" log. Paper + pencil is
   civilization's memory.
2. **Education as a job** — one person whose role is teaching the young (and adults)
   the ladder. Every skill mastered must be *taught*, or it's lost in one generation.
3. **Apprenticeship** — pair a master and a student for each critical trade:
   smithing, pottery, medicine, agriculture.
4. **Dispute resolution** — a defined, fair process. Feuds destroy communities faster
   than famine does. Simple rules, consistently applied, beat complex ones applied
   arbitrarily.
5. **Emergency plan** — who does what in fire, flood, attack, or illness.

## Making knowledge survive

- **Redundancy**: three people know every critical skill, not one.
- **Physical backup**: write critical guides on durable media (this is exactly what
  this website is for — print it).
- **Inventories**: track seed, medicine, fuel, and tools — know what you have before
  it runs out.

## The trap of "we'll remember"

Oral memory degrades in 2–3 generations. If it's not written down, it's temporary.
Writing is the difference between a society and a camp.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Does your community keep written records of its knowledge and decisions?
- [ ] Is every critical skill taught to more than one person?
- [ ] Do you have a fair, known process for disputes?
- [ ] Do you have a written emergency plan and inventory?
