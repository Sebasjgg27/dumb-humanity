# Roads & Bridges

A road is a machine for moving weight cheaply. Where goods must travel (see
[transport](../transport/)), a proper road cuts effort, time, and
breakage by a factor you can feel in your boots. Bridges make rivers and valleys
obstacles instead of borders. This is the physical backbone of A3 — and most of it is
brute craft, not magic.

## Building a road that lasts

The Roman formula, still unbeaten:

1. **Clear and level the route** — pick the easiest gradient; a road that climbs less
   moves more. Survey it first (see [optics_instruments](../optics_instruments/)).
2. **Drain the foundation** — water is the road's enemy. Camber the surface (a gentle
   crown) so water sheds; dig side ditches to carry it away.
3. **Build in layers**:
   - Base: large stones, rammed.
   - Mid: smaller gravel or crushed rock, rolled.
   - Wearing surface: fine gravel/sand, compacted. (Crushed stone interlocks — the
     secret of every hard road ever built.)
4. **Keep it crowned and clear** — maintenance is a schedule, not an emergency.
   Fill potholes while they're small.

**Grade** matters more than surface: a 1-in-20 slope is comfortable for a cart; steeper
slopes exhaust animals and wash out. Switchback long hills rather than climbing them
straight.

## Bridges

A bridge is the sum of its weakest span. Match the design to what you can build:

- **Log/timber beam** — spans up to ~10 m. Simple; keep the deck drained and replaced.
- **Stone arch** — the immortal bridge: spans tens of meters, needs no maintenance for
  centuries if the foundations hold. Build on rock or deep piles, not on mud.
- **Suspension (cable)** — iron wire or chains (see
  [wire_drawing](../wire_drawing/)) span the longest distances; the
  deck hangs from a catenary. Towers, anchorages, and deck stiffness are the details
  that prevent the famous failure modes (buckling, galloping).

**Golden rules**: never span a flood river low and narrow; always build piers on
foundations below scour depth; and load-test every bridge before regular traffic.

## Salvage and repair

- **Existing roads**: clear, drain, and resurface rather than rebuild — the line is
  already surveyed.
- **Salvaged asphalt** can be patched cold and rolled.
- **Repair priorities**: farm-to-market first, mine-to-forge second, village-to-village
  third. Roads earn their keep by the ton, not the mile.

## Safety

- Roadworks are dangerous: flag traffic, keep people clear of machinery, and bridge
  work needs a harness over water.
- Load-test bridges with a heavy cart before people crowd them.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Can you build a drained, crowned, layered road?
- [ ] Can you cross a river with a sound beam or arch bridge?
- [ ] Do you maintain roads on a schedule, not in emergencies?
- [ ] Have you load-tested every new bridge?
