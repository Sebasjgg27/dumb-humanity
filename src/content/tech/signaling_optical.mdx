# Optical Signaling

Before radio, the fastest long-distance communication was **light and sight**: flags,
arms, and sun flashes on hilltop relay stations. A chain of towers can pass a message
across a region at the speed of a glance — and it needs no electricity, no wire, and no
infrastructure beyond hills, lenses, and trained operators. It is the honest precursor
that keeps working when the radio doesn't.

## The principle

Every optical system is a **line-of-sight relay chain**:

1. **Stations** on high points, spaced so each can see the next (roughly 10–40 km by
   hilltop, more by tower).
2. A **code** — messages reduced to signals.
3. **Operators** who read, record, and re-transmit.

Signal stations built for war once stretched across continents. The same model
reconnects a region now.

## Three practical systems

**1. Semaphore (mechanical):**
- A mast with movable arms; each arm position is a letter or number.
- A telescope (see [optics_instruments](/dumb-humanity/tech/optics_instruments)) reads
  the next station; a codebook maps arm positions to messages.

**2. Flag signaling:**
- Two flags, one in each hand; positions encode letters (the naval alphabet).
- Fast, portable, needs no tower — only visibility.

**3. Heliograph (sun flashes):**
- A mirror catches sunlight and flashes it toward the next station, keyed on and off
  (mirror shutter) like Morse.
- Morse code (see [radio](/dumb-humanity/tech/radio)) is the natural message format:
  short and long flashes.
- **Range is astonishing** — tens of kilometers from a hilltop on a clear day.

## Building the network

1. **Pick the chain** — survey the line of hills; choose stations with the clearest
   sight lines (a telescope survey pays off).
2. **Build stations** — simple shelters for operators, mounting for the apparatus.
3. **Write the codebook** — standard phrases ("All clear", "Distress", weather, and
   routine traffic) to compress long messages.
4. **Train operators** — reading, keying, and discipline (see
   [education](/dumb-humanity/tech/education)).
5. **Schedules** — agreed hours of watch, like the
   [infrastructure_scheduling](/dumb-humanity/tech/infrastructure_scheduling) model.

## When it fails

- **Weather**: fog, rain, and haze kill sight lines. Have a fallback (riders or
  radio once it exists) and know when to stand down.
- **Night**: lamps and mirrors fail without sun — a bright oil lamp with a shutter
  (kerosene from [oil_refining](/dumb-humanity/tech/oil_refining)) extends the day.
- **Operator error**: the message is only as good as the reader; repeat back critical
  messages.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Can you pass a coded message between two hilltop stations?
- [ ] Can you use Morse-style flashing by day (mirror) and night (lamp)?
- [ ] Do you have a codebook and trained, scheduled operators?
- [ ] Have you mapped a relay chain across your region?
