# Copper Extraction

Copper is the metal of electricity. Iron builds the machines; copper conducts the
current that powers them. Every future wire, motor, and circuit needs copper —
which is why its recovery matters long before it can be mined fresh.

## Sources, ranked by ease

1. **Native copper** — pure copper chunks found in certain regions (e.g., Great Lakes).
   Hammer and shape directly, no smelting.
2. **Malachite / azurite** — bright green/blue ores, easy to spot on outcrops.
3. **Scavenged copper** — the modern world is a copper mine: pipe, wire, motor
   windings, plumbing fixtures. Recover and re-melt.
4. Roasted sulfide ores (chalcopyrite) — the professional route, much harder
   (arsenic/SO2 issues).

## Smelting copper

1. Roast the ore (drive off sulfur/water).
2. Charge furnace with ore + charcoal, blast with bellows, reach ~1085°C.
3. Copper **melts** (unlike iron's bloom) — tap liquid copper, let it solidify.
4. **Casting**: pour into clay/stone molds — ingots, then wire, tools, and vessel shapes.

## Why copper for electricity (not iron)

Copper's electrical conductivity is ~6x iron's, and it doesn't rust away your
connections. The [wire drawing](../wire_drawing/) and
[electricity](../electricity_generator/) nodes depend entirely on
a reliable copper supply.

## The copper alloy ladder

- Pure copper: soft, ductile — good for wire, poor for cutting tools.
- **Bronze** (copper + tin): see [bronze](../bronze_working/).
- Brass (copper + zinc): bearing surfaces, valves.
- Copper's real destiny on this ladder is as *conductor*, not weapon.
