# Charcoal: The Industrial Fuel

Charcoal is the quiet giant of the whole ladder. Wood burns at ~300–500°C — too cool
for pottery glazing, glass, or metal. Charcoal, which is nearly pure carbon, burns
at **900–1400°C** with a clean, concentrated flame. Every furnace, forge, and crucible
from here to the blast furnace runs on it. Get charcoal right first.

## The principle

Heating wood in a sealed, low-oxygen environment drives off water and volatile gases,
leaving carbon. It's *not* burning the wood — it's cooking it. When the process is
done wrong you get ashes; done right you get a bag of high-grade fuel.

## Method 1: The earth mound (no drum needed)

1. Stack split hardwood in a dome, ~1–1.5m across, leaving a small central chimney.
2. Pack the whole dome under a 15–20cm layer of turf/earth/mud.
3. Light the chimney. Let it burn until the outer layer shows glowing — then seal
   the chimney and every crack with mud.
4. The dome cooks internally for **12–48 hours**, driven by the heat of its own
   combustion with almost no oxygen.
5. When smoke turns from white to thin blue, the process is done. Unseal, let cool,
   and harvest charcoal. 
6. **Risk**: if you let air in too early, the whole batch ignites and burns to ash.

## Method 2: The steel drum kiln (reliable, repeatable)

A sealed 55-gallon drum with a few small air holes at the base and a loose lid is far
more controllable than a mound — and repeatable. Light a fire under it, cook 4–8 hours,
then seal completely and cool.

## Quality checklist

- **Good charcoal**: black, shiny, rings like a bell when tapped, breaks with a clean
  snap, leaves almost no ash.
- **Bad charcoal**: soft, dull, ashy, low heat — it was burned, not cooked.
- Weight ratio: 1kg of good charcoal from ~6–8kg of dry wood is realistic.

## Uses that follow

- **Furnace fuel** — the only thing hot enough for iron and copper.
- **Filter material** — crushed charcoal filters water (see [water](/dumb-humanity/tech/water)).
- **Char cloth** — smolder-stable fire tinder.
- **Gunpowder** — one of the three ingredients (see [gunpowder](/dumb-humanity/tech/gunpowder)).

## Safety

- Charcoal produces **carbon monoxide** — never burn or cook it indoors without venting.
- Sealed-drum batches can pressure-pop; keep a distance and vent slowly.
