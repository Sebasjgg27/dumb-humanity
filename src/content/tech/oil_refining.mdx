# Oil Refining (Kerosene & Lubricants)

Crude oil is a mix of different hydrocarbons, and that mix is the point: boiled
carefully, it separates into **kerosene** (clean lamp fuel), **lubricants** (the blood
of every machine), **diesel** (later, for engines), and **asphalt** (roofs and roads).
The first target of recovery oil-refining is *not* gasoline — it's **kerosene and oil**.

## Why these fractions matter

- **Kerosene**: burns clean and bright in a simple lamp — better light than candle or
  rush, far less smoke. It replaces whale oil and tallow. Lamps, lighthouses, and night
  work depend on it.
- **Lubricating oil & grease**: a machine economy *grinds to a halt* without oil.
  Steam engines, machine tools, and rail axles all demand continuous lubrication.
  This is often the single most limiting consumable.
- **Asphalt**: waterproofing, roads, roofing.

## Fractional distillation

1. Heat crude oil in a **boiler** — it must never be a sealed vessel under pressure.
2. Vapors rise into a tall **column** that is cooler at the top.
3. The **lightest** fractions condense highest (gasoline, naphtha); **kerosene**
   condenses mid-column; heavier oils condense below; **asphalt** stays in the boiler.
4. Draw each fraction off into its own receiver.

Approximate boiling ranges: gasoline 30–200 °C · kerosene 150–300 °C · diesel/heating
oil 250–350 °C · lubricants 350 °C+ · residue = asphalt.

A simple pot-still (see [distillation](../distillation/)) recovers the
fractions in stages instead of continuously — crude but workable.

## Cleaning the fuel (the acid wash)

Crude kerosene burns smoky and deposits gum unless cleaned:

1. **Acid wash**: mix with a small amount of concentrated sulfuric acid (see
   [sulfuric_acid](../sulfuric_acid/)); it reacts with sulfur compounds
   and gum-forming impurities.
2. Settle, drain the acid layer.
3. **Water wash**, then a **clay/sand filter** (fuller's earth) to polish it.

The result is bright, clean-burning fuel.

## Safety (this is a fire factory)

- Oil vapors are **explosive** — no flame near the still, no sparks, enormous
  ventilation.
- Never let a still run unattended; never boil a sealed vessel.
- Kerosene and gasoline stored only in proper sealed metal containers, away from
  buildings.
- Oil fires need foam/sand/water-fog, never a water jet on burning oil.
- Do this work far from living quarters, downwind.

## A word on gasoline

Do not chase gasoline yet — there's nothing at this level that burns it, and it's
volatile and dangerous to store. Kerosene and lubricants are the A3 prize. Diesel
matters only when compression engines return, much later.
