# Batteries: Storing Electricity

Generators make electricity, but *storage* makes it useful — light when the wheel
stops, power for a radio when the steam is off. The lead-acid battery (1859!) is
the A2 workhorse, and it's buildable from A2 materials: lead, dilute sulfuric acid,
and jars.

## The lead-acid cell

- Two plates of lead in dilute [sulfuric acid](../sulfuric_acid/).
- Charging (from a generator/DC source) drives a chemical reaction storing energy.
- Discharging reverses it, delivering current. It's a reversible chemical pump.
- Multiple cells in series (6 cells ≈ 12V) = a usable battery bank.

## Build notes

- Lead plates: cast or roll pure lead; the paste (PbO₂ on positive, spongy lead on
  negative) forms during repeated charge/discharge cycles — "forming" takes time.
- Jar: glass or glazed ceramic (acid-proof). Wood spacers keep plates apart.
- Acid strength: roughly 35% — from the [battery acid recovery](../sulfuric_acid/) method.
- Capacity is set by plate surface area — bigger plates, more storage.

## The zinc-carbon cell (simpler, no acid)

Zinc cup + carbon rod + ammonium chloride paste (or saltwater). Weaker but much
safer and easier — fine for small devices.

## What stored power unlocks

- **Radio/telegraph** operation off-grid (see [radio](../radio/)).
- **Night lighting** from a charged bank.
- **Electroplating** (see [electroplating]).
- **Electric instruments** in medicine and science.

## Care and danger

- Acid: gloves/goggles. Never tip a cell; acid spills burn.
- Hydrogen gas bubbles during charging — vent the room, no open flames.
- Never short a battery (rapid, dangerous discharge/fire).
- Dead cells can often be **rebuilt** rather than replaced — lead is reusable.
