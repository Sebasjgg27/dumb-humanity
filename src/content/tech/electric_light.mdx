# Electric Light

Night is expensive — it costs fuel and eyes. Electric light is the first product of
electricity that changes daily life: it extends working hours, shrinks the fire risk of
indoor flames, and proves the electrical system to everyone in town. It is also the
simplest electrical device to build from scratch.

## The core problem

To make light with electricity you must make something **white-hot** without it burning
up. There are two honest paths:

1. **The arc lamp** — a spark gap between carbon rods. Blinding, simple, no vacuum needed.
2. **The incandescent bulb** — a filament glowing in an air-free bulb. The classic.

## Path 1: Carbon arc lamp (easiest first light)

1. Mount two **carbon rods** (from a battery or a smith's forge) end-to-end with a small gap.
2. Connect a DC generator across them.
3. Close the gap briefly to start the arc; it sustains itself once running.
4. It burns bright and hot — the rods erode, so a mechanism (even hand-cranked) feeds
   them together.

**Cautions:** the light is harsh and the arc is a fire and burn hazard. Set it high,
shield it, and never look straight at it. Requires decent generator output.

## Path 2: Incandescent bulb (the real goal)

1. **Make a filament** — carbonize a cotton thread or bamboo splint by baking it in a
   sealed tube until it becomes a carbon conductor (a smith's charcoal heat works).
2. **Seal it in a glass bulb** with a narrow neck (see [glassblowing](../glassblowing/)).
3. **Evacuate the bulb** — get the air out with a vacuum pump, or the filament burns in
   seconds. This is the hard step; a good vacuum makes the bulb live for hours instead
   of seconds.
4. Seal the neck shut around the lead-in wires.

Run it from the generator. Carbon filaments glow warm and steady. (Tungsten — far
better — comes much later on the ladder.)

## Why the vacuum is everything

In air, white-hot carbon reacts with oxygen and burns away almost instantly. Removing
the air (or filling with a noble/inert gas) is what makes the bulb last. If you cannot
make a good vacuum yet, stick to the arc lamp.

## Safety

- Generator output at these levels can shock; dry hands, insulated handles, no bare
  splices near metal.
- Bulbs are hot and fragile — glass shards and burns.
- Arc lamps are a genuine fire risk: no combustible material overhead, water, cloth,
  or fuel nearby.
- Never open a running circuit "to see" — that's how people get hurt.

## Uses on the ladder

- **Factories**: light lets workshops run in shifts, doubling output of the
  [machine_tools](../machine_tools/) shop.
- **Public light** at safe distances: fewer injuries, fewer fires.
- **Signaling** and lighthouses at harbor edges.
