# Metrology: Standards, Weights & Measures

**Industry without measurement is just craft.** You can't make two identical
interchangeable parts, calibrate a lathe, or build a steam engine that seals, without
agreed, reproducible units. Metrology is the quiet gate that every precision technology
sits behind. A meter, a kilogram, a degree, a second — defined from nature, rebuilt
from scratch.

## Rebuilding units from nature (the reboot-safe way)

| Unit   | Rebuild from                              |
| ------ | ----------------------------------------- |
| Meter  | 1/10,000,000 of Earth's quadrant (impractical) — better: use a person's stride, then *fix* a physical bar and declare it |
| Second | A pendulum of ~0.994m swings at ~1s per swing |
| Kilogram | A liter (volume) of water ≈ 1kg — a fixed metal cylinder = the standard |
| Liter  | A cube of 0.1m per side                    |
| Degree (temp) | Water freezes = 0, boils = 100 (Celsius) |
| Degree (angle) | 1/360 of a circle — the ancients had this right |

The trick isn't the exact value — it's **that the standard is fixed, written down,
and replicated** so everyone measures the same way.

## The measurement tools to build (in order)

1. **Straightedge & square** — file two bars flat and square against each other.
2. **Dividers & compass** — for transferring lengths and drawing circles.
3. **The standard bar** (the "meter" of your society) — a steel bar, filed, declared,
   protected.
4. **Screw threads as measurement** — a screw of known pitch becomes a micrometer:
   one turn = one exact pitch. This is the mechanical ruler.
5. **Scales** — a beam balance with fixed brass weights, calibrated against a liter
   of water (1kg).

## Why this unlocks everything

- **Lathe work**: cutting to dimension requires measuring to 0.1mm or better.
- **Steam engine**: piston-and-cylinder need round, true, uniform bores.
- **Clockwork**: gears only mesh if tooth spacing is exact.
- **Electricity**: consistent units for voltage/current/coils.

## The culture of measurement

- Write your standards down. Put them in a protected, dry place.
- Calibrate tools *against the standard* regularly.
- In a reboot, precision is a social achievement — everyone measures the same.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Do you have a fixed, recorded standard of length, mass, and time?
- [ ] Can you make a straightedge and square that pass a repeatability test?
- [ ] Can you file/grind a screw of known pitch for measurement?
- [ ] Do your artisans agree on the same units?
