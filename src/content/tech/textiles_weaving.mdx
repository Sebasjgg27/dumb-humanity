# Textiles & Weaving

Clothing, sacks, blankets, sailcloth, and rope all come from one chain: **fiber →
spin → weave**. Textiles are also a survival currency — cloth is scarce, portable,
and always needed. Spinning and weaving are the first "manufacturing" — converting
raw material to standardized product.

## Spinning: fiber → thread

1. **Prepare fiber**: wool — wash, card (comb into alignment). Flax — ret + scutch
   (separate fibers). Cotton — gin by hand (slowly!).
2. **The drop spindle**: a stick with a whorl (weight). Twist the fiber into yarn
   while the spindle hangs and spins. 
3. **Drafting** (feeding fiber smoothly) is the skill; the spindle does the twist.
4. Consistency: even twist + even thickness = yarn that weaves well.

## Weaving: thread → cloth

- **Frame loom**: a rectangular frame with warp threads stretched lengthwise; the
  weft is passed over and under across it. A shed stick and heddle bar speed it up.
- **Belt/backstrap loom**: simplest portable loom — the classic.
- A **tablet-weave** band loom produces the strongest strap/sling webbing.
- The first useful products: bags, straps, blankets, and sailcloth — not fancy cloth.

## The wool chain (full cycle)

Shear → wash → card → spin → knit/weave → full (shrink/felt) → use. Every step is
learnable and the finished wool garment outlasts the winter you build it in.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Can you spin usable, even yarn from local or scavenged fiber?
- [ ] Can you weave a bag or blanket on a frame loom?
- [ ] Do you have a working source of fiber (animals, plants, salvage)?
- [ ] Can you make cloth for clothing, storage, and sails?
