# Steel Making & Heat Treatment

Iron is a building material; steel is a *tool* material. The difference is a few
fractions of a percent of carbon — and the heat treatment that turns soft metal into
a razor or a spring. Steel is what makes precision (and thus the whole industrial
ladder) physically possible.

## The carbon story

- Pure iron (wrought) is soft and tough — bends, doesn't break.
- Add carbon (0.2–2%): the iron gets harder, and — with the right cooling — can
  become knife-hard.

## Methods to make steel

1. **Cementation**: pack wrought iron strips in charcoal, seal in a chest, heat
  for days at ~1000°C. Carbon diffuses into the iron's surface → "blister steel."
2. **Crucible steel**: melt blister steel with fluxes in a clay crucible → uniform,
  high-quality steel (the best pre-industrial method).
3. **The bloomery route**: stop the iron bloom short of pure, leaving carbon in —
  the oldest accidental steel.

## Heat treatment (the skill that makes a blade)

| Stage       | How                                 | Result                     |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Anneal      | Cool slowly in ash                  | Soft, workable             |
| Hardening   | Heat cherry-red, quench in water    | Hard + brittle             |
| Temper      | Reheat to ~200-300°C (straw/blue color), cool | Hard + tough |
| Normalize   | Cool in air                         | Uniform grain              |

- **The color gauge** is the pre-thermometer standard: straw → brown → blue as
  tempering temperature rises. Straw = spring, blue = chisel.
- Tempering after hardening is the difference between a knife and shrapnel.

## What steel enables

Files, drills, chisels, springs, needles, and eventually [machine tools](
/dumb-humanity/tech/lathe) that cut other metals. The lathe *is* a hardened-steel
tool — steel makes the machine that makes the machines.
