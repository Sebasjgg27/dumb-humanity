# Science & Measurement

Measurement is science made physical. Before you can *know* that something works, you
must be able to *measure* it; before you can *share* a result, everyone must use the
same units. This guide turns the scientific method from a slogan into a working
procedure for the workshop, the field, and the clinic.

## Standard measures (the foundation)

Agree on physical standards, kept in a safe place, and copy them:

- **Length** — the meter: convenient fractions of it (the old definition was the
  millionth of a quadrant of the Earth; for recovery, just pick a preserved rod and
  copy it).
- **Mass** — the kilogram: a sealed reference weight.
- **Time** — the second, derived from the [clockwork](/dumb-humanity/tech/clockwork)
  pendulum and the rotation of the Earth.
- **Temperature** — from the [thermometers](/dumb-humanity/tech/thermometers) guide.

Protect the master standards; make working copies for daily use. When the masters wear,
renew them from the copies. This is exactly how real metrology institutes operate.

## The scientific method, as a procedure

1. **Ask a specific question** — "Does this batch of charcoal burn hotter?"
2. **Form a hypothesis** — "It does, because it was made from oak."
3. **Design a controlled test** — two batches, identical except the variable. Change
   one thing at a time.
4. **Measure** — write the numbers down. No numbers, no science.
5. **Repeat** — three, five, ten times. One lucky result is not a result.
6. **Conclude honestly** — the test says what it says, even if you hoped otherwise.
7. **Share it** — recorded so someone else can repeat it (see
   [education](/dumb-humanity/tech/education)).

## The discipline that matters most: write it down

- Keep a **workshop log**: every batch, every temperature, every failure.
- Failures are data. The log of what went wrong is often more valuable than the log of
  successes.
- Label and date everything. In ten years, the notebook is the knowledge base.

## Instruments you can build

- **Balance scales** — a beam on a pivot with pans; accurate to fractions of a gram.
- **Spring scale** — a spring's stretch measures force.
- **Volumes** — calibrated jars from standard weights and water (1 liter of water = 1 kg).
- **Thermometer, clock, compass** — covered by their own guides.

## Why this is a ladder technology

Reproducibility is the difference between a folk tradition and a science. The village
that measures is the village whose metallurgy, chemistry, and medicine *improve
generation to generation* instead of merely surviving. Measurement is how knowledge
compounds.

- [ ] Can you build an accurate balance?

- [ ] Can you design a test that changes only one variable?
