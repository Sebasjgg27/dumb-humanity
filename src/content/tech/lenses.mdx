# Lenses & Optics

Lenses are the organs of scientific vision: they let a community see germs, stars,
and distant signals. Grinding glass into a curve is one of the most *precise* crafts
on the ladder — and the skill (polishing to tolerances of fractions of a millimeter)
practices the exact discipline that A3/A4 precision manufacturing needs.

## Grinding a lens, by hand

1. **Rough**: shape a glass blank on a spinning lap (iron/lead/copper disc) with
   coarse sand + water. The lap's curve transfers to the glass.
2. **Fine**: finer abrasives (finer sand, emery) reduce roughness.
3. **Polish**: the finest stage (putty powder / rouge = iron oxide) → transparent surface.
4. **Test**: hold over print — clear magnification = done. Look at a distant light
   edge — fuzziness = more work.
5. Two-lens systems: combine a convex (magnifier) and concave (diverger) for
   telescopes and microscopes.

## The optical instrument family

| Instrument | Build                                       | Reveals |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Magnifying glass | One convex lens                      | Small details, *fire starting* |
| Microscope | Two-lens system, short focus          | Cells, bacteria, blood |
| Telescope  | Long-focus objective + eyepiece       | Astronomy, distant ground |
| Eyeglasses | Corrective lenses (scavenge > grind)  | Restored sight |

## The fire-starting bonus

A strong convex lens focuses sunlight — a magnifying glass is a dependable fire
starter that needs no bow drill (as long as the sun shines).

## Scavenge before grinding

Modern optics (eyeglasses, camera lenses, binoculars) are precision products you
can't match by hand for decades. Recover them early and *preserve them* — they are
irreplaceable for a generation.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Can you grind a lens that magnifies clearly?
- [ ] Can you assemble a working microscope or telescope?
- [ ] Have you scavenged and protected modern optics?
