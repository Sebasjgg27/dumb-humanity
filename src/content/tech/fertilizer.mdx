# Fertilizer (Nitrogen)

Agriculture without nitrogen is a ceiling. Crops eat nitrogen from the soil; harvests
remove it; and if nothing puts it back, yields collapse year after year until famine.
Before industrial ammonia synthesis, humanity's population was literally capped by
natural nitrogen. Restoring that nitrogen is how a recovering society feeds more people
than it starts with.

## The nitrogen problem, in one line

The air is 78% nitrogen, but plants can't use it as a gas — only fixed forms in the
soil. You must get nitrogen *into* the ground in a form roots can take up.

## The low-tech nitrogen sources (use them all)

1. **Compost and manure** — recycled plant and animal waste is the baseline. Nothing
   wasted; everything returned to the soil.
2. **Legume rotation** — clover, beans, and peas host bacteria that pull nitrogen from
   the air into the soil. Plant them and plow them in (green manure). This was the
   ancient nitrogen engine.
3. **Urine** — diluted and aged, a surprisingly effective nitrogen fertilizer. Every
   community generates it for free; use it on fields, not in water.
4. **Guano/bird droppings** — concentrated nitrogen and phosphate. The historic global
   fertilizer trade ran on islands of bird dung.

## The chemical route: ammonium nitrate

When organic sources aren't enough, chemistry extends them (see
[nitric_acid](/dumb-humanity/tech/nitric_acid)):

1. Collect **ammonia water** — stable waste and urine age into it; burning nitrogen-rich
   plant matter yields soot and salts rich in ammonia too.
2. Neutralize **nitric acid** with the ammonia to form **ammonium nitrate** — the world's
   classic nitrogen fertilizer.
3. **Warning**: ammonium nitrate is also an explosive (see
   [explosives](/dumb-humanity/tech/explosives)). Store it away from fuel, heat, and
   sparks; keep records.

## Phosphorus and potash (the other two)

Nitrogen alone isn't enough:

- **Phosphorus** — bone meal (ground, heated bones), ashes, and guano.
- **Potassium** — wood ashes and plant residues.

A complete fertilizer mixes all three (N-P-K), adjusted to your soil. Test by growing:
thin, yellow crops = nitrogen; weak roots and slow growth = phosphorus.

## Applying it well

- Fertilize **before and during** growth, not all at once.
- Don't overdo nitrogen — it forces leaf growth, burns roots, and washes into water.
- Combine with good drainage and crop rotation; fertilizer is not a substitute for
  soil care.
