# Soap & Hygiene

The single biggest life-saver in a reboot is not any medicine — it's **soap**. Clean
hands before meals and wound care cut infection and disease rates more than the entire
pharmacy could. Soap is also the beginner's chemistry: fat + lye = a completely new
substance. It's the perfect first chemical reaction to master.

## The chemistry, plainly

Soap = **fat + lye (alkali)**. The alkali breaks the fat apart into soap molecules:
one end loves water, the other loves grease. That's why soap washes both — it
emulsifies oil and lifts dirt.

## Making lye (potassium hydroxide / potash)

1. Collect **hardwood ash** (softwood ash is weaker). White ash from a hot fire is best.
2. Soak it in water in a barrel with a drain hole.
3. The liquid that drips out is lye water. Test it: a fresh **egg should float** in it —
   that's the right strength. Too weak = greasy soap; too strong = skin-burning soap.
4. Lye is caustic. **Handle with gloves; it can burn skin and blind eyes.**

## Making soap (cold process, simplified)

1. Render clean animal fat (tallow) — melt, strain out bits.
2. Slowly add lye water to warm fat while stirring constantly.
3. Keep stirring until it "traces" (leaves a faint line on the surface when the spoon
   is lifted).
4. Pour into molds. Wait 24h, cut, and **cure for 3–4 weeks** — uncured soap burns skin.
5. Test: a small patch on the wrist. It should feel clean, not burn.

## Hygiene doctrine

- **Hands before every meal and after every toilet** — this alone prevents most disease.
- Clean water, soap, and boiled bandages for wounds.
- Separate the "clean" food area from the "dirty" waste area.
- Bathing and clean clothes break the lice/flea typhus cycle.
- Latrines 30m+ from water sources, downhill, and covered.
