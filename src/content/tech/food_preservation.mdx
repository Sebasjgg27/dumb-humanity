# Food Preservation

Growing food is only half the job — keeping it through the dead season is what turns
a harvest into a society. Preservation is the difference between summer abundance and
winter famine. Every method here works without electricity.

## The 6 methods (ranked by reliability)

| Method      | Works for        | Shelf life | Effort |
| ----------- | ---------------- | ---------- | ------ |
| Drying      | Fruits, veg, meat | Months    | Low    |
| Salting     | Meat, fish       | Months     | Low    |
| Smoking     | Meat, fish       | Months     | Medium |
| Fermentation | Veg, dairy, grains | Months | Low |
| Root cellar | Root veg, apples | Whole winter | Low |
| Sugaring / honey | Fruit | Months | Low |

## Drying (the universal method)

Sun, air, or smoke. Cut food thin, dry it fully — **under-dried food molds**. A food
is "dried" when it cracks, not bends. Store airtight and dry. Jerky: thin meat strips,
salted and smoke-dried — a calorie-dense, portable staple.

## Fermentation (no heat, no salt needed — just patience)

Salt + water + vegetables in a sealed crock: beneficial bacteria out-compete spoilage
microbes. Sauerkraut, kimchi, and pickles preserve vegetables for months while
increasing nutrient availability. The same principle makes beer and mead — and those
preserve calories *and* sterilize water later in the ladder.

## Root cellar (the zero-energy cold chain)

A cool (0–7°C), humid, dark, ventilated pit or bank. Potatoes, carrots, apples, and
cabbage last through winter. This is A1's cold chain — A3's refrigeration is its
mechanical descendant.

## Canning with what you have

Glass jars + boiling water bath (for acidic foods) or pressure (for low-acid) preserve
almost anything. Improvised: hot-pack into jars, seal with wax or melted fat. Watch
for bulging lids = botulism risk, throw away.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Can you preserve a full season's surplus so it lasts until the next harvest?
- [ ] Do you have working methods for meat, vegetables, and grain?
- [ ] Can you keep a root cellar at the right temperature and humidity?
- [ ] Do you know which preservation failures are dangerous (bulging jars, mold)?
