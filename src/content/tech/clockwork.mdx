# Clockwork & Timekeeping

Time is the hidden infrastructure of science. A clock measures the regularity the
universe runs on — and it's the purest exercise of [metrology](
/dumb-humanity/tech/metrology) and the [lathe](/dumb-humanity/tech/lathe): gears that
mesh exactly, a spring that drives steadily, an escapement that ticks uniformly.
Without good time, navigation, astronomy, and the entire A3/A4 precision world stall.

## The parts

| Part        | Role                                              |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Power       | Falling weight or coiled spring                   |
| Geartrain   | Reduces fast rotation to one precise hour hand    |
| Escapement  | Releases the train in equal ticks (the "heart")   |
| Oscillator  | Pendulum (gravity) or balance wheel (spring) — the timekeeper |

## Build order

1. Cut and finish gears on the [lathe](/dumb-humanity/tech/lathe) — teeth must be
   uniform or the clock stops.
2. The **pendulum**: a weight on a rod; period = 2π√(length/g). A ~1m pendulum
   ticks ~1 second. Adjust length to set the rate.
3. The **escapement**: a toothed wheel + a pallet that "clicks" once per swing,
   releasing the train in equal steps.
4. The dial: gears reduce the escapement's thousands of beats/day down to one hour
   and one minute hand.

## Why clocks are science infrastructure

- **Navigation**: longitude = time difference (see [navigation]).
- **Astronomy**: measuring the sky needs a steady timebase.
- **Scheduling**: railways (A3) literally cannot run without coordinated time — 
  every A3 rail network *requires* clocks.
- **Calibration**: a good clock calibrates everything else that oscillates.
