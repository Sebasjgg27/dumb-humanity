# Optical Instruments

Lenses are not an end — they are raw material. Mounted and combined, they become the
**telescope** (see far), the **microscope** (see small), and the **surveyor's
instruments** (see precisely). These three opened modern science: the telescope found
the planets, the microscope found disease, and the surveyor's level built the works of
empire. All of it starts with the lenses from the [lenses](../lenses/)
guide.

## The telescope (see far)

The Galilean design is the simplest and needs only two lenses:

1. A **long-focus convex objective** at the front gathers light and forms an image.
2. A **short-focus concave eyepiece** magnifies it (upright image, small field).
3. Mount both in a tube with the right spacing; adjust by sliding.

The Kepler design (two convex lenses) is sharper but flips the image — fine for stars,
and the standard for astronomy. Performance is set by the *objective's* size and
quality, not the eyepiece: a bigger, better-made objective sees more.

Uses: navigation (spotting land), signaling (reading distant flags), surveying, and
astronomy — including finding your latitude from the stars (see
[navigation](../navigation/)).

## The microscope (see small)

A simple microscope is just a strong convex lens (short focal length) held close to the
eye. A **compound microscope** stacks a short-focus objective with an eyepiece:

1. The objective makes a magnified image of the specimen.
2. The eyepiece magnifies that image again.
3. Mount on a stand with a focus screw and an adjustable mirror to light the specimen.

Uses: inspecting textiles, tools, and blades for flaws; checking water for living
organisms; examining wounds and insects for infection vectors. The microscope turns
[medicine_herbal](../medicine_herbal/) from guesswork into observation.

## The surveyor's level and theodolite

To build straight and level (roads, canals, water lines — see
[infrastructure_roads](../infrastructure_roads/) and
[infrastructure_water](../infrastructure_water/)) you must measure
sights:

- **Level**: a telescope with a crosshair, mounted level — reading a graded staff
  measures height differences over distance.
- **Theodolite**: a leveled telescope that measures angles in a circle — the instrument
  that lays out everything from field boundaries to railway alignments.

These work with the [metrology](../metrology/) standards: angles are
measured against a graduated circle, distances with rods and chains.

## Making them accurate

- **Crosshairs**: spider silk or fine wire stretched across the eyepiece focal plane.
- **Focus**: a sliding tube or rack-and-pinion — fine, repeatable adjustment.
- **Rigid mounts**: wobble is the enemy; a firm tripod or stand is half the accuracy.
- **Protection**: optics are fragile; store lenses in padded cases, dry, and handle by
  the edges.
