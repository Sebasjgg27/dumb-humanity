# Signaling & Rescue

A reboot group must be findable. Whether it's rescue aircraft in the early days or a
neighboring community later, the ability to broadcast "people here" is infrastructure.
The three signal families: **visual (day), visual (night), and sound**.

## Daytime signals

- **Smoke**: a smoky fire (green leaves on hot coals) during the day. Three puffs
  in a group pattern = the distress signal.
- **Signal mirror**: aim a reflected flash at a distant object — the flash is visible
  for miles in daylight. Practice the thumb-hole aiming method.
- **Ground signals**: large contrasting shapes visible from the air. The international
  patterns: **V = need assistance, X = need medical help, ⟲ = proceed this way**.
- **Bright tarps / marker** arranged in open ground.

## Nighttime signals

- **Fire triangle or line**: three fires in a triangle, or one long line — the standard
  distress layout for aircraft.
- **Flare or flashlight**: a sequence of three flashes, pause, three flashes.

## Sound signals

- **Whistle**: three short blasts = distress (universal).
- Respond to three signals with three signals.
- Rescue crews look for: movement, contrast, and **reflectivity**.

## Radio as a later signal (A2)

The first radio transmitters in a reboot community serve this same function — emergency
beaconing over distance. See [radio](../radio/).

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Can you make a distress fire and smoke signal in under 10 minutes?
- [ ] Can you aim a signal mirror accurately at a target 2km away?
- [ ] Do you know the international ground and whistle patterns?
- [ ] Does every adult in your group know the three-blast whistle signal?
