# Iron Smelting (Bloomery)

Iron is the technology that the entire industrial tree hangs on. When you hold the
first bloom — a lump of metal spongey from the furnace — you hold the mother of all
machine tools, plows, rails, and engines. This node is the hard gate to A2.

## Ore: the first problem

You need an ore rich in iron oxide, and the easiest source is often **scrap**.

- **Bog iron**: rusty nodules from iron-rich wetlands — ancient Europe's ore.
- **Hematite / magnetite / limonite**: found in outcrops, old mines, riverbanks.
- **Scavenged steel**: rebar, beams, old vehicles — re-melt instead of smelt. Smelting
  from ore teaches the chemistry; scrap gives you metal today.

Test: crush, wet, and look for rusty color. Strong magnet test for magnetite. A
high-phosphorus or high-sulfur ore makes brittle iron — quality matters.

## The bloomery furnace

1. A clay cylinder ~60–100cm tall with a small hearth and a **tuyère port** at the
   base. Walls 5–10cm thick, refractory clay.
2. Alternate layers of charcoal and crushed ore, charged from the top.
3. Blast air through the tuyère with [bellows](../bellows/) — 1150°C+
   is the target.
4. The iron never melts fully — it forms a **bloom** (spongy metal + slag) at the
   bottom. The slag is the waste; the iron is the prize.
5. Tap out, let cool, break the furnace, retrieve the bloom.

## Consolidation (the second half of smelting)

A bloom is spongy, not usable metal. **Hot-forge it**: reheat red-hot, hammer it
repeatedly to squeeze out slag and weld the pores shut. This forge-welding step is
where raw smelting becomes *wrought iron*.

## The fire is everything

- Every temperature failure is an air-supply failure. More blast, better fuel.
- Pre-warm the furnace, dry your charcoal, keep the tuyère clear of ash/clogging.

## From iron → steel → tools

- Wrought iron (soft, tough) → [steel](../steel_making/) by adding
  carbon → hardening and tempering for edges.
- First products that change everything: **plow blades, axes, hammers, tongs,
  chisels, files, needles** — each one a multiplier for every other trade.

## Safety (this is the most dangerous node so far)

- Molten slag + hot metal + CO-rich gases. **Extreme** care required.
- No water near the furnace (steam explosions). Heat-protective clothing, tongs,
  and a clear, dry work zone.
- Never pour/move hot metal without a plan.

- [ ] Can you produce a workable iron bloom and consolidate it by forging?
