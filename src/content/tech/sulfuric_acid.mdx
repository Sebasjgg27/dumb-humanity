# Sulfuric Acid (The King of Chemicals)

If iron is the mother of machines, **sulfuric acid** is the mother of chemicals.
Almost every industrial chemical process touches it: it's the electrolyte of batteries,
the acid that makes nitric acid, the reagent that makes fertilizers, explosives, and
pharmaceuticals. On this ladder, mastering sulfuric acid is how the chemistry tree
opens.

## The dangerous shortcut: scavenge first

Modern ruins give you *dilute* sulfuric acid in every car battery (~35%). Collect
battery electrolyte, filter it, and **evaporate gently** to concentrate it. This is
the fastest path to concentrated acid in a reboot — treat every step as high-risk.

## The lead chamber process (pre-industrial, doable)

1. Burn **sulfur** (from pyrite ore, or recovered deposits) to make SO₂ gas.
2. Mix with air and water vapor in a big lead-lined chamber with **nitrous oxides**
   (from the nitric acid reaction) as a catalyst.
3. The gases react into sulfuric acid mist, which condenses on the chamber walls.
4. Drain and concentrate by gentle evaporation.
5. **Lead is essential** (lead survives the acid); if no lead, use glass/ceramic
   apparatus in small scale.

## The iron sulfate route (older, simpler to control)

Roast **iron pyrite (FeS₂)** → drives off SO₂ → oxidize to SO₃ → dissolve in water →
sulfuric acid. Slower and more laborious, but needs no lead and no catalyst tricks.

## Concentration matters

- **Dilute acid** (battery strength): batteries, electrolytes, pickling.
- **Concentrated acid**: dehydrating agent, reacts with salt/nitrates for stronger acids.
- Concentrated acid + water **generates violent heat** — always add acid *to* water,
  never water to acid.

## What it unlocks

- [Nitric acid](/dumb-humanity/tech/nitric_acid) — for fertilizers and gunpowder.
- [Batteries](/dumb-humanity/tech/batteries) — stored electricity for A2/A3.
- Fertilizers — the difference between subsistence farming and surplus.
- Oil refining and electroplating further up.

## Safety (extreme)

- Acid burns are permanent. Gloves, goggles, apron, and ventilation — always.
- Never add water to concentrated acid.
- Store in glass or heavy plastic, labeled, sealed, away from children and food.
- Neutralize spills with ash/bicarbonate, not water.
