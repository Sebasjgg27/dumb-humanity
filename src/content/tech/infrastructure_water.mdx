# Water Infrastructure

The [water](/dumb-humanity/tech/water) guide keeps a household alive. This guide keeps a
*population* alive. Safe, abundant water at a town scale is the single most
civilization-making public work: it slashes disease, enables density, and frees people
from hauling every liter by hand. It is a project that requires the institutions of
[permanent_shelter](/dumb-humanity/tech/permanent_shelter) and
[community_governance](/dumb-humanity/tech/community_governance) — water systems are
built by communities, not individuals.

## The gravity model (the Roman secret)

If your water source sits **higher** than the town, let gravity do the work:

1. **Source**: a spring, lake, or river intake, protected from pollution.
2. **Conduit**: a gently sloping channel, pipe, or aqueduct carries the water downhill.
3. **Storage**: an elevated reservoir (a tower or hillside tank) creates pressure.
4. **Distribution**: a branched network of pipes delivers to public taps, then houses.

Pressure comes from height: **10 meters of head ≈ 1 atmosphere** of pressure. An
elevated tank is a battery for water.

## Laying the network

- **Pipes**: clay (fired, waterproofed), wooden (bored logs or staves), stone channels,
  or iron once smelting allows. Start with public taps at crossroads before plumbing
  houses — public taps serve more people per meter of pipe.
- **Gradients**: slope distribution pipes slightly so they drain; keep supply pipes
  below frost line or insulated.
- **Valves**: shut-off points at every branch so one leak doesn't empty the town.
- **Filtration**: a sand/gravel filter bed before the storage tank polishes the water;
  boiling remains the rule for drinking (see [water](/dumb-humanity/tech/water)).

## Pumps: when gravity isn't enough

- **Water-wheel pumps** — a wheel powered by the river raises water into the reservoir.
- **Steam pumps** — the [steam_engine](/dumb-humanity/tech/steam_engine) becomes the
  workhorse that lifts water from wells and rivers. (Early steam engines existed almost
  entirely to pump mines and towns dry.)

## The other half: sewage

What goes down must go *away*:

- **Sanitary sewers**: waste flows downhill in closed channels, flushed by water, to a
  disposal point **downstream and away** from any intake.
- **Never** put an intake downstream of a discharge. The cardinal rule of public health.
- Latrine pits, sealed and emptied periodically, serve areas before sewers reach them
  (see [sanitation](/dumb-humanity/tech/sanitation)).

## Governance and records

- Water systems need **a responsible authority**, budgets, and labor allocation —
  this is the [community_governance](/dumb-humanity/tech/community_governance) test.
- **Record the layout**: maps, pipe sizes, and valve locations, printed and archived
  (this is where [printing](/dumb-humanity/tech/printing) pays off). An unrecorded
  system is a mystery to its own operators in ten years.

## Safety and maintenance

- Protect the intake and reservoir from animals, runoff, and tampering.
- Inspect and flush lines on a schedule; repair leaks fast — a silent leak is a
  flooded foundation later.
- Chlorination (small amounts of bleach) is a later-stage polish; for now, filtration
  and boiling.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Is your intake upstream of all waste discharge?
- [ ] Can you deliver safe water to public taps by gravity?
- [ ] Can you pump water when gravity fails?
- [ ] Do you have maps, valve records, and a maintenance authority?
