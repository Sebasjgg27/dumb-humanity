# Clothing & Thermal Protection

Clothing is portable shelter. Before textiles, humans used hides — and the hide-working
skills (scraping, tanning, sewing) are the direct ancestors of modern leather and textile
industry. The layer principle beats any single garment.

## The layer system (works in every era)

1. **Base layer** – against skin, wicks moisture (wool > cotton, which chills when wet).
2. **Insulation layer** – traps still air (wool, down, fiberfill, dry grass between cloth).
3. **Shell layer** – blocks wind and rain (waxed cloth, oilskin, leather, plastic sheet).

## Hide processing (rawhide → leather)

1. **De-flesh**: scrape meat and fat off with a stone scraper. Missed flesh rots.
2. **De-hair**: soak, then scrape the grain side; or pit-tan with wood ash + water.
3. **Sew** edges together with an awl-punched holes and sinew thread (bark fiber works).
4. **Tan** to stop it turning stiff/rotten:
   - **Brain tan** (traditional): brains mashed with water, rubbed in, then smoked.
   - **Bark tan**: soak in oak/bark tannin water for weeks.
   - **Smoke tanning**: suspends the leather over a smoky fire — soft, waterproof, rot-resistant.

## Shoes

Feet fail first. Moccasins from a single folded hide + sinew stitching, or woven
grass sandals. Always have two pairs and dry them by the fire. Frostbite from wet
feet disables people silently.

## Scavenge is king at A0

Modern ruins are clothing factories. Blankets, down jackets, wool coats, boots, and
foam padding are worth more per gram than most manufactured goods. Re-harvest and
re-sew rather than starting from hides when possible.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Do you have 3 functional layers for your climate?
- [ ] Can you process a hide into leather that won't rot?
- [ ] Can you sew a functional garment with bone awl + sinew?
