# Sanitation & Waste Management

Waste is not just unpleasant — it's the vector for cholera, dysentery, typhoid, and
hepatitis. A community that manages its waste survives; one that doesn't, dies of
diarrhea. Sanitation is public health at the community scale, and it's almost free.

## The golden rules

1. **Latrines downhill and ≥30m from any water source.**
2. **Cover after use** (flies carry feces → food → mouth).
3. **Wash hands with soap** after the latrine, before food, before wound care.
4. **Contain, don't spread** — never dump raw sewage into streams used downstream.

## The pit latrine (right way)

- Dig 1.5–2m deep, narrow at the top.
- A raised seat (logs, a slab) keeps distance between people and flies.
- A tight-fitting lid. Vent pipe with a screen if you have one.
- When full, backfill with soil and dig a new one — never empty a pit latrine.
- In a settlement, space latrines around the edge, not clustered.

## Composting toilet (makes soil, not sewage)

Sawdust/ash added after each use gives a carbon layer that drives thermophilic
composting — after ~1 year it's safe, sterile compost. This is a closed loop:
waste in, garden soil out.

## Greywater

Kitchen and wash water (no feces) can irrigate non-edible plants or a wetland filter.
Keep it moving; standing greywater breeds mosquitoes.

## Medical waste

Bandages, needles, and dressings are disease vectors. Burn them or bury them deep,
away from water. Never reuse needles without boiling (A2 stainless/glass syringes).
