# Radio (Wireless Telegraphy)

Radio is the technology that ends the isolation of distance: messages travel at the
speed of light, over mountains and oceans, with no wire and no road. For a recovering
civilization it is **coordination infrastructure** — warnings, schedules, distress
calls, and news that turn a scatter of towns into a connected society.

## How it works (the bare bones)

An oscillating electric current in a wire radiates **electromagnetic waves** that travel
through air. A distant wire picks up a tiny echo of that wave, and a detector turns it
back into a signal you can hear. The first practical radios were crude but worked.

## The transmitter: the spark gap

The simplest transmitter is a **spark**:

1. Build up high voltage (from a generator — a transformer or induction coil helps).
2. Discharge it across a gap — the spark radiates a burst of radio noise.
3. Key it on and off (a telegraph key) to send **Morse code**: short and long bursts.

A spark transmitter is brute force — it sprays signal across the whole spectrum and
interferes with everything nearby — but it works, and range with a good antenna is
surprisingly large. It is the honest first step, exactly as history did it.

## The antenna (half the battle)

- Make it **long** (tens of meters if possible) and **high** (on a mast or tree).
- A vertical wire with a "counterpoise" or ground connection performs best.
- Range scales with antenna and power: a tall antenna on a hill is worth more than
  more power.

## The receiver: two honest options

**The coherer (simplest):**
- A small glass tube of loose metal filings between two electrodes.
- A radio pulse makes the filings "cohere" and conduct; a relay clicks.
- Tap the tube after each pulse to reset. Crude but classic.

**The crystal detector (better):**
- A **galena** (lead sulfide) crystal with a thin wire ("cat's whisker") resting on it
  acts as a rectifier — it turns the radio wave into a pulsing current you can hear in
  high-impedance earphones. No power needed for the detector itself.

## The operating system: Morse code

Radio telegraphy runs on Morse — memorized, not written:

- Short ("dit") and long ("dah") — the entire alphabet, numbers, and procedural signs.
- Standard distress: **SOS** (... --- ...).
- Train operators, maintain log books, and standardize call signs.

## Building the network

1. **Relay stations** — repeat signals over mountains by receiving and re-keying.
2. **Schedules** — agreed times to listen and send.
3. **Priority rules** — distress first, then weather, then routine traffic.
4. **Written records** — every message logged; radio is memory, not conversation.

## Safety and honesty

- High-voltage spark transmitters can kill. Build, insulate, and guard them properly.
- Keep antenna wires clear of power lines and away from foot traffic (they can carry
  dangerous induced currents).
- A spark transmitter interferes with everyone on the band — use minimum power and
  minimum time. Later, vacuum tubes (see [vacuum_tubes](/dumb-humanity/tech/vacuum_tubes))
  will make clean, tuned, powerful transmitters — the real radio.
