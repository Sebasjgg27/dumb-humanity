# Vacuum Tubes

In a vacuum, a hot wire emits electrons, and those electrons can be steered. That
behavior gives you two miracles: **rectification** (turn AC into DC) and
**amplification** (let a whisper control a roar). Vacuum tubes are the door between
"electricity" and "electronics" — the first radios, telephones, and computers all ran
on them.

## The physics, simply

Heat a metal filament until it glows: it throws off electrons. In air they'd instantly
burn and be blocked; **in a vacuum** they fly freely toward a positively charged plate.
What happens next depends on how many electrodes you add:

- **Diode (2 electrodes)** — filament + plate. Current flows one way only:
  **rectification**. Turns the generator's AC into usable DC.
- **Triode (3 electrodes)** — add a wire grid between filament and plate. A small
  voltage on the grid controls a large current to the plate:
  **amplification**. This is the invention that made electronics possible.

## Making a tube (the honest challenge)

1. **Blow the glass** envelope — a bulb or cylinder with the electrodes sealed
   through the base (see [glassblowing](../glassblowing/)).
2. **Assemble the internals**: a filament (carbon or tungsten wire), the plate, and
   for a triode the grid — supported on mica or glass spacers.
3. **Evacuate** — a vacuum pump draws the air out through a side tube. The quality of
   the vacuum decides the tube's life; poor vacuum = short, unreliable tube.
4. **Seal** the exhaust tube with a torch.

Each step is a craft; expect weeks of rejects before a working tube. That is normal —
the first tube builders had exactly the same failure rate.

## What you can build with them

- **Rectifier tubes** — turn AC generator output into DC for
  [electroplating](../electroplating/) and steady circuits.
- **Amplifier tubes** — take the faint signal from a
  [radio](../radio/) receiver and drive earphones or speakers; extend
  telephone lines with repeater amplifiers.
- **Oscillator tubes** — clean continuous-wave transmission replacing the crude spark
  gap; this is when radio becomes *real*.

## Working safely with tubes

- Tubes run hot (the filament is white-hot) and operate at **dangerous voltages**.
  Guard terminals; keep metal away; work one hand in pocket.
- Glass tubes break into sharp pieces; hot glass burns.
- Salvaged tubes are fine — just test them (they may be dead or gassy).

## The road ahead

Diode → triode → multi-electrode tubes → radio telephony → and eventually the
transistor that replaces all of this. That is the A4 gate. For A3, the triode is
enough to change everything.
