# Railway & Bulk Transport

The railway is what makes a *region* — not just a village — function. A single
steam locomotive moves more freight in a day than a hundred carts. Rail is also the
A3 infrastructure backbone: canals are slow, roads wear out, but a rail line is a
permanent artery. And rails are **the best-scavenged item in the modern ruins** —
they don't rot.

## The components

1. **Trackbed**: graded, drained, compacted ground — flatness is everything.
2. **Ties** (sleepers): timber laid across the trackbed.
3. **Rails**: scavenged steel rail, or rolled/forged iron — laid end-to-end, spiked
   to ties, aligned to gauge.
4. **Locomotive**: the [steam engine](../steam_engine/) on wheels —
   boiler, cylinders, drive wheels, and the boiler safety discipline.
5. **Gauge**: one fixed width for all lines (the [metrology](../metrology/)
   standard made physical). Mixing gauges breaks networks.

## What rail does for the ladder

- **Moves ore and fuel** to furnaces — iron smelting at scale becomes possible.
- **Moves food and people** — regional food security.
- **Spurs development**: villages on the line grow; towns off it wither.
- A1's [animal draft](../animal_domestication/) rails (horse-drawn
  wagonways) work before steam exists — build the track first, add steam later.

## Safety

- Rails need regular inspection (cracks, spreading, washouts).
- Boiler explosions on locomotives are catastrophic — same rules as [steam](../steam_engine/): safety valves, hydro-testing, never over-pressure.
- Track worker visibility: a lone worker on a live line is a corpse. One person
  flagging, always.

## Validation checklist

- [ ] Can you lay and align rail to a consistent gauge over a kilometer?
- [ ] Can you run a steam locomotive without boiler incidents?
- [ ] Can you move bulk freight (ore, grain, fuel) at 10x cart capacity?
- [ ] Have you surveyed the best-scavenged rail assets in your ruins?
