CUANDO LA AYUDA ESTÁ LEJOS

Primeros Auxilios

Conocimiento médico crítico cuando la ayuda está lejos.

Wound Care & Bleeding

How to stop bleeding and prevent infection in any environment.

Pasos

  1. 1 Apply direct pressure with the cleanest cloth available
  2. 2 Elevate the injured area above the heart if possible
  3. 3 Pack deep wounds tightly with clean cloth
  4. 4 Secure bandage firmly but not so tight it cuts circulation
  5. 5 Clean wound with boiled and cooled water when bleeding stops
  6. 6 Change dressing daily or whenever it becomes wet/dirty

Advertencias

  • Do not remove objects embedded in a wound — stabilize around them
  • Watch for signs of infection: redness, swelling, warmth, pus, red streaks
  • Tourniquet is last resort only — mark the time on the patient

Fractures & Splinting

How to immobilize a broken bone with improvised materials.

Pasos

  1. 1 Do not try to reset the bone — splint in the position found
  2. 2 Find rigid material: sticks, rolled newspapers, cardboard, tent poles
  3. 3 Pad the splint with cloth for comfort
  4. 4 Tie splint above and below the fracture point
  5. 5 Check circulation below the splint (pulse, feeling, color)
  6. 6 Immobilize the joint above AND below the fracture

Advertencias

  • A compound fracture (bone through skin) needs wound care FIRST
  • Check distal pulse every 30 minutes — tighten splint if circulation is lost
  • Splint should be tight enough to prevent movement but not cut off blood flow

Hypothermia Treatment

Recognizing and treating dangerous body temperature loss.

Pasos

  1. 1 Remove wet clothing immediately — replace with dry layers
  2. 2 Warm the core first: chest, neck, head, groin (NOT extremities)
  3. 3 Use skin-to-skin contact in a sleeping bag as last resort
  4. 4 Give warm (not hot) sweet drinks if conscious — no alcohol
  5. 5 Handle the person gently — rough movement can cause cardiac arrest
  6. 6 Monitor breathing — shallow breathing may stop without warning

Advertencias

  • Do NOT rub or massage extremities — causes cold blood to rush to the core
  • Do NOT give food to someone with severe hypothermia
  • Afterdrop: core temp continues falling after rewarming begins
  • Severe hypothermia is irreversible — prevention is everything

Snakebite Response

What to do (and not do) when bitten by a venomous snake.

Pasos

  1. 1 Move away from the snake to prevent a second bite
  2. 2 Keep the bite below heart level — immobilize the limb
  3. 3 Remove rings, watches, and tight clothing near the bite (swelling)
  4. 4 Mark the edge of swelling and write the time
  5. 5 Get to medical help as fast as possible — this is time-critical
  6. 6 Note the snake description if possible for antivenom selection

Advertencias

  • Do NOT cut the wound or try to suck out venom
  • Do NOT apply a tourniquet — this concentrates venom and causes tissue death
  • Do NOT apply ice — this worsens tissue damage
  • Do NOT give aspirin or ibuprofen — they thin the blood

Burn Treatment

Treating thermal, chemical, and sunburns in the field.

Pasos

  1. 1 Cool the burn under clean running water for 10-20 minutes
  2. 2 Remove jewelry and tight clothing from the burned area
  3. 3 Cover loosely with a clean, non-stick dressing
  4. 4 Give pain relief if available (ibuprofen or paracetamol)
  5. 5 Keep the patient hydrated — burns cause significant fluid loss
  6. 6 Change dressings daily, watching for infection

Advertencias

  • Do NOT use ice, butter, or toothpaste on burns
  • Do NOT pop blisters — they are a natural sterile dressing
  • Chemical burns: flush with water for 20+ minutes, remove contaminated clothing
  • Electrical burns: entry wound may be small but internal damage can be severe
