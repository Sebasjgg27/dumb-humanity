Animal Domestication

Animals convert calories people can’t eat (grass, scraps, waste grain) into calories people can — and into wool, leather, milk, eggs, manure, and eventually draft power. The draft animal is the A1-to-A2 bridge: it multiplies one farmer’s muscle by ten.

Which animals, what they give

AnimalProductsA-level of real value
ChickenEggs (fast), meatA1 — eggs are daily protein
PigMeat, fat, manureA1

CattleMilk, meat, leatherA2 (plow/draft power)
Horse/Donkey/OxDraft power, transportA2

The quiet rules of livestock

  • Manure is fertilizer, not waste — the link that keeps farm soil fertile without synthetic chemicals.
  • Breeding: keep the best-natured, most productive animals for breeding — this is how domestication compounds over generations. Never eat your breeding stock.
  • Rotate pastures or their parasites and diseases build up.
  • Fence before acquiring animals, not after.

Draft power (A2)

A single ox or horse can plow in a day what a person does in a week. The plow upgrade and the railway both start with animal traction. Draft animals are the muscle that industrializes agriculture before engines exist.

Validation checklist

  • Do your animals have secure fencing and adequate feed for the dead season?
  • Are you breeding for temperament and productivity, not just meat?
  • Is their manure being composted into your fields?
  • Do you protect breeding stock from slaughter?
